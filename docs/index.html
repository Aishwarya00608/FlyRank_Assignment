1. Introduction & Problem Statement

In enterprise digital publishing and e-commerce architectures, content inventories routinely scale into tens of thousands of indexed URLs. While acquiring initial search engine rankings requires substantial capital and editorial investment, maintaining organic visibility against algorithmic volatility, competitor content updates, and shifting query intent represents an equally critical operational hurdle.

Currently, enterprise search teams rely on crude heuristic rules to identify decaying content—most commonly, arbitrary position-drift thresholds (e.g., "any URL dropping past position 15") or simple month-over-month aggregate traffic comparisons. These legacy heuristics suffer from severe systemic deficiencies:

  • High False Discovery Rates: Standard rank drops often reflect momentary SERP volatility or non-commercial long-tail query variance rather than genuine deterioration of the page's core visibility.
  • Severe Resource Misallocation: Content operations have finite bandwidth (e.g., 10 to 30 deep page refreshes per bi-weekly sprint). Wasting editorial capacity on false alarms creates an opportunity cost that leaves truly decaying, high-yield assets unaddressed.
  • Lagging Rather Than Leading Detection: Waiting for gross traffic collapse means intervention occurs after authority has severely eroded, requiring substantially more effort to recover SERP prominence.

The Decision It Supports: This research directly supports the weekly editorial triage decision: ranking enterprise content assets by their statistical probability of subsequent decay and pairing each flag with a specific diagnostic reason code to guide intervention type.

2. Data Architecture & Sanitization Safeguards

This study leverages the official FlyRank warehouse release (internship-warehouse) accessed via DuckDB over Hugging Face. The empirical evaluation focuses on granular daily performance records within the partition fact_content_daily_performance_sample.parquet, joined against content-level metadata across a 30-day continuous observation window (June 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026).

Data Ingestion & Cohort Filtering

To ensure high statistical power while filtering out uninformative noise, strict inclusion criteria were enforced:

  • Verified Telemetry Flag: Retained only observations where active Google Search Console integration was verified (gsc_data_available = true).
  • Minimum Traffic Floor: Assets were required to exhibit at least 20 organic impressions during the baseline feature window (imp_prev15 ≥ 20). This eliminates the long tail of dormant or freshly indexed pages whose variance is non-stationary.
  • Cohort Volume: The final clean cohort yielded 123,896 unique content items across diverse enterprise client profiles.

3. Methodology & Leakage Prevention

The fundamental risk in operational search forecasting is lookahead data leakage—where future performance metrics or aggregate summary statistics inadvertently contaminate the training features. To ensure rigorous real-world validity, a time-partitioned evaluation design was formulated.

Time-Aware Partitioning Scheme

To prevent temporal lookahead bias, all data ingestion was partitioned around a strict temporal boundary at June 15, 2026, 23:59:59 UTC:

Window 1: Historical Telemetry
Feature Window (Days 1–15)
June 1, 2026 – June 15, 2026

Predictive feature inputs are computed exclusively from this 15-day period. Models have zero exposure to subsequent dates.

Window 2: Ground-Truth Outcome
Evaluation Window (Days 16–30)
June 16, 2026 – June 30, 2026

Future organic trajectory is evaluated here to verify if impressions drop by ≥ 20% relative to baseline.

Feature Engineering Matrix

5. Limitations & Honest Analytical Framing

Analytical Governance Disclaimer: In strict alignment with research reproducibility and academic rigor, the findings of this capstone are subject to the following structural bounds:
  • Observed Statistical Association, Not Algorithmic Reverse-Engineering: This model measures empirical correlations within historical performance telemetry. It does not claim to decode, replicate, or prove Google Search's ranking algorithms or patents. Furthermore, it cannot guarantee that executing a content refresh will causally reverse organic decline.
  • Short-Horizon Observational Window: The 30-day continuous dataset captures immediate momentum shifts. It does not account for multi-month macroeconomic seasonality (e.g., annual holiday surges), macro industry cycles, or multi-week Google Core Updates that alter broad SERP layouts.
  • Omission of Off-Page & Technical Confounders: The telemetry ingested is restricted to Search Console performance. It excludes critical off-page and infrastructure covariates such as loss of external referring backlinks, technical indexing bloat, server-side 5xx errors, Core Web Vitals degradation, or direct competitor publication bursts.
  • Decision-Support Scope: All model outputs, probability scores, and reason codes are designed strictly as an evidence-backed triage queue to aid human editorial workflows. They are not deterministic instructions for automated mass-republishing.
